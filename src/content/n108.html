When not to mock

  • Don't mock what you own and can run cheaply: pure functions, value objects, your own in-process modules.
  • Don't mock types you don't own (mocking the Postgres driver's API). Wrap them in your own interface and fake that, or use a real instance (chapter 106).
  • Tests that assert on every internal call break with every refactor even when behaviour is unchanged. Prefer asserting on outcomes (state, return values) over interactions.
  • Needing many mocks for one test means the unit has too many dependencies: a design smell (chapter 37).

Practice

108.1 think A unit test mocks 6 collaborators and breaks every time you refactor. What does that tell you, and how do you fix it?

The unit has too many dependencies and the test is checking interactions rather than outcomes. Split the unit, use fakes for its own boundaries, and assert on results and state.

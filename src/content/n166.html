Hardening container images and runtimes.

  • Minimal images (distroless, slim, scratch): fewer packages means fewer known vulnerabilities (CVEs), and no shell for an attacker to use.
  • Scan images in CI and in the registry: trivy image ghcr.io/ujjwal/shop-api:sha. Fail builds on critical vulnerabilities that have a fix available. Rebuild regularly to pick up base-image patches.
  • Runtime: non-root user, read-only root filesystem, --cap-drop ALL, no-new-privileges, seccomp defaults, no --privileged, no host network or PID namespace, and never mount the Docker socket.
  • Resource limits stop a compromised or buggy container from exhausting the host.
  • Sign and verify: cosign-signed images, with an admission policy (Kyverno, Sigstore policy-controller) that only lets signed images from your registry run.
  • Runtime detection: Falco alerts on suspicious behaviour, such as a shell spawned inside a container, or writes to /etc.

Practice

166.1 basic Scan one of your images with trivy image. How many critical vulnerabilities have a fix available, and does a newer base image remove them?

Usually most go away just by rebuilding on the latest slim or distroless base. Add trivy to CI with --severity CRITICAL --ignore-unfixed --exit-code 1.

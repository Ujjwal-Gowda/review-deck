Convolutional networks: the architecture that made computer vision work.

  • Convolution: a small filter (e.g. 3×3) slides across the image, computing dot products. It detects local patterns wherever they appear (translation equivariance) and shares weights across positions, so it needs far fewer parameters than a dense layer on raw pixels.
  • Hierarchy: early layers detect edges, middle layers textures and parts, deep layers objects.
  • Pooling / strides shrink the spatial size and grow the receptive field.
  • Landmark architectures: LeNet → AlexNet (2012, the GPU breakthrough) → VGG → ResNet (residual connections) → EfficientNet. Vision Transformers (ViT) now rival or beat CNNs at scale.
  • Transfer learning: take a pretrained model (ResNet or ViT trained on ImageNet), replace the final layer, and fine-tune on a few hundred of your own images (e.g. "fresh vs spoiled produce"). This is the practical default.
  • Tasks: classification, detection (YOLO), segmentation, OCR.

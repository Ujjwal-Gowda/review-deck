Planning for losing a server, a region, or a whole account.

MetricQuestionExample
RPO (recovery point objective)how much data can we afford to lose?5 minutes of orders
RTO (recovery time objective)how long can we be down?1 hour for the POS, 1 day for reports
StrategyRTOCost
Backup and restorehourslow
Pilot light (a minimal copy running elsewhere, data replicated)tens of minutesmedium
Warm standby (a scaled-down full copy)minuteshigher
Active-active in several regions≈ 0highest, plus data-consistency complexity
  • Match the strategy to each system's RPO/RTO. Not everything needs active-active.
  • Write a runbook (step-by-step recovery instructions) and rehearse it (game days). An untested disaster recovery plan is a hope, not a plan.
  • Remember the non-technical parts: who decides to fail over, how customers get told, and whether the shop can run offline. A POS that keeps selling offline and syncs later is disaster recovery built into the product.

Practice

153.1 think Set RPO and RTO for the FreshOn POS, online orders and monthly reports, and pick a disaster-recovery strategy for each.

POS: offline mode plus sync gives RPO ≈ 0 for sales and an RTO of minutes. Online orders: warm standby or fast restore, RPO in minutes. Reports: backup and restore, RTO of a day.

Reading a flame graph

The x-axis is the share of samples (not time order); the y-axis is stack depth. Wide bars are where the time goes. Look for wide "plateaus" at the top: functions doing the work themselves. Common findings: JSON serialisation, regex compilation inside loops, logging, and lock waits.

  • Profile realistic workloads (production-like data, under load), not toy inputs.
  • Distinguish CPU time from wall-clock time: an I/O-bound service shows little CPU. Use tracing for that (chapter 156).
  • Continuous profiling in production (Pyroscope, Parca) catches regressions after deploys.

186.1 basic Profile a slow Python script with py-spy and produce a flame graph. What's the widest frame?

py-spy record -o out.svg -- python3 script.py. The widest top frame is where to optimise first: often parsing, regex, or a quadratic loop.

Defining "reliable enough" in numbers, and using the gap to decide when to ship versus when to stabilise.

  • Availability multiplies: a request that needs 3 services each at 99.9% succeeds only 99.7% of the time.
  • Budget policy: budget remaining means ship features; budget exhausted means freeze risky launches and spend time on reliability. This turns "move fast" vs "be stable" into data instead of an argument.
  • Don't aim for 100%: users can't tell 99.99% from 100% (their own Wi-Fi is worse), and every extra nine costs roughly 10× more.
  • Burn rate: how fast the budget is being spent. At 14× burn, a 30-day budget is gone in about 2 days, so that should page someone.

Practice

161.1 basic Your SLO is 99.9% of successful checkouts over 30 days, and you handle 3M checkouts a month. How many failures can you afford, and what burn rate should page?

Budget = 3,000 failures. At a 14.4× burn rate, the budget is gone in about 2 days, so page at that rate over the last hour; file a ticket at around 3× over 6 hours.

The outputs of a build, and treating them as immutable, traceable things.

An artifact is anything a pipeline produces for later use: container images, compiled binaries, dist/ bundles, test reports, coverage, SBOMs.

  • Build once, promote many: the same artifact (identified by its digest) goes to staging, then production. Rebuilding for production risks shipping something different from what you tested.
  • Traceability: embed the commit SHA and version (-ldflags "-X main.version=$SHA", image labels) and expose it at /version, so you always know what's running.
  • Storage: images in registries; binaries in GitHub Releases or S3; CI artifacts (actions/upload-artifact) are short-lived by default.
  • Retention: keep releases; expire per-commit builds after N days.
  • Provenance: SBOMs (syft), signatures (cosign) and SLSA attestations prove what went into an artifact and who built it.

Practice

122.1 basic Embed the git SHA into a built binary or bundle, expose it at /version, and verify it after deploying.

Go: -ldflags "-X main.version=$(git rev-parse HEAD)". Vite: define: {__SHA__: JSON.stringify(sha)}. Then curl /version should match the deployed commit.

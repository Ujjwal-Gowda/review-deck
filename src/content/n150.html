DNS as production infrastructure: naming services, migrating safely, failing over.

  • Buy the domain at a registrar, run DNS at a good provider (Cloudflare, Route 53). Point the registrar's NS records at the provider. Turn on registrar lock and 2FA: losing the domain loses everything.
  • Name services by role: api., app., status., files.. Point them at load balancers or CDNs via CNAME/ALIAS, never at individual servers.
  • Migrations: lower the TTL to 60–300 s a day ahead, switch the record, watch traffic move, then raise the TTL again.
  • DNS-based failover and routing: health-checked records (Route 53 failover, Cloudflare load balancing) answer with a healthy endpoint; latency or geo routing sends users to the nearest region. Clients cache answers, so failover is never instant.
  • Email records (SPF, DKIM, DMARC) decide whether your invoices and OTP emails land in the inbox or in spam.
  • CAA records restrict which certificate authorities may issue certificates for your domain.
  • Keep DNS as code (Terraform, OctoDNS) so changes are reviewed and reversible.
